Malaysian Case Study

Cassava (Ubi Kayu) Detoxification Rates

In Malaysia, Cassava (Manihot esculenta) is an important climate-resilient crop supporting local food security. However, raw cassava contains cyanogenic glucosides (linamarin) that release toxic **hydrogen cyanide (HCN)** when disrupted.

To make safe flour or food products, roots undergo an aqueous fermentation/soaking process. According to realistic Malaysian processing baselines, a standard 24-hour soaking cycle drops HCN concentrations from a dangerous 120 mg/kg down towards safer levels. Understanding the rate of change at specific hours helps food technologists optimize processing windows and conserve water resource cycles.

The Mathematical Model

The detoxification curve follows a first-order decay model stabilized by traditional lingering bounds, approximated for our 24-hour setup by the function $f(x)$:

Concentration Function:
f(x) = 105 × e-0.15x + 15
Where x = soaking time (hours), f(x) = HCN concentration (mg/kg)
First Derivative (Rate of Change):
f'(x) = -15.75 × e-0.15x
Yields the instant detoxification velocity in mg/kg per hour at hour x
*By applying the chain rule to $f(x)$, the constant 15 drops out, and the exponent coefficient (-0.15) multiplies the leading 105 to produce the localized rate vector.
Data Source & Reference Standards: Modeled after safety parameters cited via the Malaysian Agricultural Research and Development Institute (MARDI) processing archives and baseline HCN tolerances monitored by the Ministry of Health (MOH) Malaysia Food Safety and Quality Division.
